How the number is built

For every transaction where a submitted wallet's v1 balance changed, the counter-value on the other side of that same transaction is measured: ETH sent, ETH returned internally, gas paid, and any transfer of a token priced below.

net loss = spent − received − value still held

Acquisitions add to spent, including the gas paid to make the purchase. Disposals add to received, net of the gas paid to sell. Transfers with no counter-value — airdrops, wallet-to-wallet sends, liquidity-pool moves — are counted at zero and flagged unpriced, because the chain alone cannot tell what they were worth to you.

Only wrapped ETH is matched by its actual contract address, and it's priced at that specific transaction's own rate — never today's — so a trade from months ago isn't valued at current prices. Stablecoins are matched by symbol alone, since a token can name itself "USDC" without being it. Symbol-only matches are still priced — dropping them would understate real swaps — but flagged symbol-matched so that isn't mistaken for a verified read. A transaction dated before the explorer's price history starts falls back to the current ETH rate and is flagged est. rate. A wallet with more history than one read can retrieve is flagged history incomplete, meaning its number is a floor, not the final word — recalculating later may raise it. A leg is only called unpriced when no counter-value could be found at all; a real net cost or gain — a sale whose gas exceeded its proceeds, for instance — is folded into the total rather than dropped just because it came out negative.

A batched transaction that bundles the v1 trade with unrelated swaps can misattribute value between them; this is rare on simple router trades but worth knowing about for anything wallet-shaped rather than person-shaped.

Per wallet, the calculator pulls its full transaction list, all token transfers, and all internal transfers in a handful of requests total — not one request per transaction — so a wallet with hundreds of transfers reads about as fast as one with a handful. Multiple wallets are read in parallel. Everything is cached in your browser and can be recomputed from scratch at any time.

Prices used for valuation (USD)

v1 is retired and priced at zero by default, so anything a wallet still holds counts fully as loss. Change either figure and the register recalculates.

Quote assets recognised

Submissions are public. Anyone can enter any address, so an entry records interest in a wallet, not ownership of it. Any wallet that bought v1 after the retirement notice is ineligible, even if it also held v1 earlier. This register reports what the chain shows; it is not a promise of repayment, and it is not financial or tax advice.
